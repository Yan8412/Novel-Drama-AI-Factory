# Spatial evidence review

For the implicated object/actor, separate three kinds of information: visible pixels, approved spatial relations, and candidate design assumptions. A labeled top view represents those known relations; it is not permission to fill unknown space.

For a visibility explanation, identify the object's persistent world position, its height/depth relation to landmarks, the camera path, and the projected boundary or occluder. Check the frozen ending view and the opening interval of any already approved following shot. A later crop cannot explain an earlier visible-ground interval.

If a local projection model is available, vary the uncertain camera/depth parameters over a justified range and record the assumptions. Without a justified error bound, a positive pixel margin remains `UNVERIFIED`; do not call it a safety margin. Do not default to building a complete scene when local evidence suffices.

Preserve reference roles and inheritance/exclusions. A pose reference cannot silently replace an identity reference, establish an unseen support, or prove that the provider accepts another control mode. For incompatible endpoint instructions, give the exact relation requiring review rather than altering the asset.

When evidence is missing, mark the explanation unverified. "Not visible" does not prove absence, and a plausible occlusion is not established fact.
